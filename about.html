A history of care · Nilambur, Kerala

Rooted in service.
Guided by Ayurveda.

The Nilambur Vaidyasala is an Ayurvedic dispensary and pharmacy whose story began with a response to the needs of local residents.

Our Origin

The Nilambur Vaidyasala opened on the 31st of Chingom, 1101 M.E., approximately 1925 C.E. It was established by His Highness Rama Varma, the 7th Prince of Cochin, in response to the healthcare needs of people in the region. The institution operated under the patronage and sole proprietorship of the Senior Raja of Nilambur. Nilambur Palace provided its building rent-free, giving the Vaidyasala a place from which to serve the community.

Its beginnings joined local need with the support of the Nilambur royal household. That history remains central to the identity of the Vaidyasala.

Why Nilambur

The region’s rich growth of medicinal herbs made Nilambur a fitting home for an Ayurvedic dispensary. Remedies were prepared from available medicinal plants according to authoritative Ayurvedic scriptures. This connection between place, plants and classical knowledge shaped the work of the Vaidyasala.

What We Do

The Vaidyasala’s work brought consultation and pharmacy together. Its services included prescribing treatment, dispensing drugs, and preparing and selling medicines. Each part of this work supported the practical needs of people seeking Ayurvedic care.

Our Speciality

Historical accounts describe the treatment of poisoning cases as a speciality of the Vaidyasala. These included snake, spider, scorpion, rat and wasp bites, as well as mad-dog bites. The dispensary also treated ordinary illnesses, including malaria, spleen enlargement and skin diseases. These descriptions belong to the Vaidyasala’s historical record.

Our Values

Consultation was offered free of charge to all. Prices were kept moderate, and treatment was provided free to people who were poor. Medicines were prepared with care in accordance with classical Ayurvedic texts. These principles expressed the Vaidyasala’s commitment to making its services accessible to the people it served.

Our Heritage in Numbers

In the first reporting period, from 1102 to 1104 M.E., the Vaidyasala recorded care for 1,185 ordinary patients and 78 poisoning cases. Its work began with a capital of Rs. 750. These early figures offer a measure of the service provided in the institution’s first years.
